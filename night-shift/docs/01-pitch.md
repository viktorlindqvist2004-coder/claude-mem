# Night Shift — Part 1: Northlight Galleria

> **Genre** Co-op survival horror, 1–4 players, first person
> **Platform** Roblox (PC, console, mobile)
> **Length** ~75 minutes across five nights, built to be replayed
> **Hook** Two authorities tell you two different things. Only one of them wants you to leave.

## The pitch

Northlight Galleria opened in 1986. In the atrium stands the Great Clock, salvaged
from a railway station that closed in 1971. Nobody has ever seen it move. It reads
**3:33**.

You and up to three friends have just been hired as night crew. You mop, you
restock, you pull the shutters down, you take the bins to the loading dock. Your
manager talks to you over the PA and the walkie. He is friendly, helpful, and
always has an answer. You never meet him.

Taped inside the staff-room locker is a handwritten list: **Rules for Night Staff**,
signed *Nora*, dated 1998. Some of her rules contradict what the Night Manager
tells you. Some of her rules have been crossed out — by her. And from the second
night onward, new lines appear in handwriting that is not hers.

The whole game is one question: **which of them do you believe, and can you prove it
before 6:00?**

## What you actually do

- **Work.** Every night has a real task list. Doing your job is not filler — it is
  camouflage. The mall wants staff. Staff are invisible. Curiosity is what gets noticed.
- **Read the rules.** They are load-bearing. Obey one and nothing happens. Break one
  and the mall takes an interest in you.
- **Test the contradictions.** Once a night, the Night Manager says something the list
  says is a lie. Both claims are checkable in the world. The team has to decide, act,
  and live with it — the choice carries forward to Night 5.
- **Talk.** Proximity voice in person, walkie-talkie across floors. The walkie is loud,
  and loud is dangerous.

## The five nights

1. **First Shift** — the rules are real.
2. **The Mannequins** — they move when nobody is looking. Somebody has to watch.
3. **Night Customers** — the mall is open. Some of the customers are not people.
4. **Blackout** — the floor plan stops holding still, and one of you has to navigate
   from the camera room.
5. **Closing** — run the closing routine that was never finished in 1998, and let four
   people go home.

## Why it works

The horror is not a monster in a vent. It is a building that has decided you are staff,
a manager who is kind right up until you try to leave, and a list written by a woman
who knew she was not going to make it and spent her last weeks writing instructions
for whoever came next.

The player-facing fantasy is **a boring job that is quietly wrong**, which is the most
reliable horror engine there is — and it gives us an excuse to render a mall at 3am in
punishing, photoreal detail.

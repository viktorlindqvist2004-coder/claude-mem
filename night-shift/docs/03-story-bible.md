# Story Bible — Northlight Galleria

> Canon reference. If a design decision contradicts this document, this document is
> wrong and should be edited — but it should be edited, not ignored.

---

## 1. Premise in one paragraph

A railway station clock that spent seventy years telling people when to leave was moved
into a shopping mall that never closes. The man who wound that clock for forty-one years
died beside it on the night the station shut, and he came with it. He does not think of
himself as a ghost. He thinks of himself as staff. A building that is open needs staff,
and staff is the only thing he has ever been, so the Northlight Galleria will stay open
forever and it will always, always need people on nights.

## 2b. The Great Clock

What everyone sees is a **1986 digital display**: four red seven-segment digits and a
colon, about 2.4 m across, in a dark panel on the front of a brass and iron housing on a
stone plinth in the middle of the atrium. It reads **03:33**.

What is inside the housing is a **1904 turret movement from Northmoor Station**, which
Halvard bought at auction and never restored, because a Victorian dial did not suit a mall
that had just opened. He cased it in and bolted the future to the front of it.

**The dedication plate naming Elias Wren is behind that panel.** This is why Malik's tape
says he could not read it, and why getting it off on Night 5 is an act rather than a
glance.

A friendly modern surface bolted over something old that nobody ever dealt with. The mall
did to the clock precisely what the Night Manager does to himself, and neither of them
knows it.

Every other display in the building runs off the same master clock — the tills, the time
clock, the vending machines, the camera room timestamps. They can all be made to agree at
once, which is worse than any of them alone.

The clock's own chapter, and the thirty-three strikes, are in
[`12-the-strike.md`](12-the-strike.md).

## 3. The mall

**Northlight Galleria, Northmoor.** Opened 1986. Four floors around a glass-roofed
atrium, though the directory only ever lists three, and the lifts only ever offered two
above ground.

- **Lower Ground** — loading dock, plant rooms, the electrical cupboard, staff corridor,
  the drained fountain's pump room.
- **Ground** — the supermarket, the atrium, the Great Clock, the main entrance shutter.
- **Floor 1** — fashion, the mannequin windows, the custodial store, the mirror wall
  outside the fitting rooms.
- **Floor 2** — the food court, the security office, the camera room, the stockroom, the
  staff room.
- **Floor 3** — there is no Floor 3. The lift panel has a worn ring where a third button
  used to be, and the 1998 paper directory lists four units on it. The manager's office
  is up there.

The architecture is 1986 Nordic municipal-commercial: terrazzo, brushed steel handrails,
smoked glass balustrades, beige laminate shopfronts, brown carpet tiles in the corridors,
painted cinderblock behind every staff door. Nothing has been refurbished since 1994,
including the signage font.

The building's tells, in order of subtlety:
1. Displays. Every display in the building reads 03:33 if you look at it twice — the
   tills, the time clock, the vending machines, the microwave in the staff room.
2. The fountain, running, when the fountain has been dry since 1991.
3. Corridors whose length does not match the floor plan on the wall.
4. A shop that is on the wrong side of the atrium from where it was an hour ago.
5. The third floor.

## 4. The Night Manager

He is a voice on the PA and on channel 2 of the walkie. He is never seen, never
rendered, and never given a body, with one exception on Night 5 — and even then he is
only ever a *silhouette that exists where a flashlight beam is*, never outside it.

**What he believes.** That he is the night manager. That the building is open. That the
crew is his, and that keeping them is a kindness, because the alternative is that they go
out into a world where nobody needs them at three in the morning.

**How he talks.** Warm, unhurried, slightly formal, the register of a man who was trained
in 1930s public service announcements and has spent decades softening it into management
speak. He compliments work specifically, not generally. He never raises his voice until
Night 5. He answers every question except one.

**The thing he never does.** He never tells you the time. Not once, across all five
nights, under any phrasing. He will redirect, joke, apologise for the tannoy, ask you a
question back. He will not say a number. This is Rule 7, it is the first hard evidence in
the game, and it is not decorative: a station clerk who cannot tell you the time is a
station clerk who knows what time it is and cannot bear to say it.

**Voice arc across the five nights.**

| Night | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Clean PA. Slight room reverb. Genuinely helpful. |
| 2 | Occasional drift in pitch. He starts using crew members' first names before they have given them. |
| 3 | The PA carries a second, quieter room behind him — a large hard space with a draught in it. He contradicts the rules directly for the first time. |
| 4 | He is on the walkie while the walkie is off. He repeats a sentence one of the players said three minutes earlier, in their voice, then continues in his own. |
| 5 | No processing at all. He is not coming out of speakers. He is in the room, and the only thing that still sounds like a PA is the word "attention". |

**His real name is Elias Wren,** and he has not heard it since 1971.

**Nothing in the game ever states this.** It is the answer to the mystery, and the crew has
to reach it from the evidence — see [`09-the-investigation.md`](09-the-investigation.md).
The one thing he does every single night, in a shopping centre, is talk like a railway
announcer, and for four nights nobody notices.

## 5. The four

They are not ghosts in the horror sense. They are staff who never clocked out. They
appear as **shades** on Night 5 only — light-coloured, indistinct, and moving like people
finishing a job.

### NORA — Checkout
Joined 1996, twenty-three. Methodical, dry, impatient with people who will not write
things down. She started the list because she worked out, early, that the building
punished the same mistakes repeatedly and rewarded nobody for learning them. She tested
her own rules and crossed out the ones that failed. She wrote the list for whoever came
after her and she was right to.
**Her tag hangs in the checkout lane she worked.**

### LO — Custodial
Joined 1997, thirty-one. Quiet, observant, on the floor more than anyone. Lo noticed the
mannequins first because Lo was the only one who cleaned the window displays and so was
the only one who ever counted them. Fond of Nora, sceptical of the list, came round to it
late.
**Their tag is on the red-collared mannequin, and returning it to the window is how you
lose it.**

### WALT — Security
Joined 1994, fifty-six. Ex-transport police. The one who worked out what the clock was,
and the one who got the solution exactly backwards. He believed that a clock that had
stopped time could be un-stopped by breaking it. He cut the power, opened the movement,
and froze all four of them at 03:33.
His last recorded words on the security tape are *"tell them to stop opening"* — which
the crew will misread as a warning about the mall's doors for four nights, and which
actually means *stop opening the building; close it properly; that is all it ever
needed.*
**His tag is on the security office desk.**

### MALIK — Stockroom
Joined 1997, twenty-six. Curious, funny, the one who read the 1986 planning documents in
the back of the stock office and found the auction receipt for the clock. He is the one
who figured out the truth, and the one who got the least time to use it. He is the voice
on Tape 4 and the reason the crew can finish the job.
**His tag is on Floor 3, outside the manager's office, because he got further than anyone
before him.**

## 5b. The living

The four are the story. These four are the *case*, and three of them look guiltier than the
answer does.

### DS RUTH CALDER — Northmoor Police
Fifty. In 1998 she was the twenty-two-year-old probationer on the callout, and she has never
let the file go. Dry, tired, competent, and wrong. She contacts the crew after Night 1
because the night-staffing contract changed hands and for the first time in twenty-eight
years somebody went in and came out.

She holds the 1998 working theory — that Walt did something to the other three and ran —
and she defends it well, because on the evidence available to a police officer it is the
correct conclusion. Convincing her otherwise is the last thing the crew has to do.

She never solves it. She corroborates, argues, pulls records, and is the reason evidence
from outside one building can enter a game set entirely inside it.

### ERIC HALVARD — Developer, 1943–1994
Built the Galleria, bought the clock, signed the 1989 complaint off as a prank from a hotel
in Oslo, and died of a stroke in his own food court in 1994. The tidiest available villain
and not the answer. His recorded voice exists on an opening-day cassette, and it sounds
nothing like the PA.

### MARGARET VALE — Centre Manager, 1986–1998
Eighty-one now, in a care home in Northmoor, entirely lucid. Buried the complaints, fitted
the lock, redated the 1998 incident report, kept the rota running after four people vanished
off it, and in 1987 took Floor 3 off the directory because she had been on it. Guilty of
eleven years of cover-up and nothing else, and the only person in the story who will simply
tell you the truth if asked.

### IVOR KASK — "Night Manager", six weeks in 1989
On the payroll for six weeks in spring 1989, paid in cash, no address, and out of focus in
every copy of the staff photograph. Looks exactly like the answer. Is, in fact, the first
witness — he filed the complaint and then he left, which is more than anyone else managed.
There is a postcard from him in the lost property drawer, Gothenburg, 1992, and finding it
is one of the better small moments in the game.

### Forged lines

From Night 2, new lines appear. They are in neat, evenly spaced print with no crossings
out, and they always argue for compliance.

- **N2** — *"12b. The red collar is only a collar. Put her back with the others."*
- **N3** — *"7b. Rule 7 was a joke between friends. Don't be rude to management."*

Five cassettes, one per night, played on the stockroom boombox or the security deck.
Playing one takes 50–70 seconds of real time, is audible through two rooms, and raises
Attention. They are worth it.

## 8. Night-by-night story beats

The mechanics are in [`04-game-design.md`](04-game-design.md); this is what the *story*
does on each night.

**Night 1 — First Shift.** Establish an ordinary, slightly grim job, a boss worth liking,
and a list worth taking seriously. He is right about three things and lies once, about
the basement, at 03:00. The lie is small and checkable. NORA's tag is beside the fuse
box at 03:33 and it is the first thing in the game that is unambiguously *wrong*.

**Night 2 — The Mannequins.** Establish that the building edits itself, including the
list. He asks the crew to put the mannequins back. The list says not to put back the red
collar. This is the first time his instruction and her instruction are both actionable at
the same moment, and it is the first forged line. LO's tag is around its neck.

**Night 3 — Night Customers.** Establish that he wants *more* staff, not just these four.
The false customers are the mall's recruiting: they are what happens to people who serve
them. At 03:33 the queue's last customer finally reaches the front and he is a security
guard in a 1994 uniform who pays with his own name tag and says one sentence. At 05:00 the
Night Manager attacks the list directly for the first time — *"That paper in the staff
room is twenty-eight years old. Bin it."* — and the crew realise he can read it.

**Night 4 — Blackout.** Establish the truth and the geography. The mall stops pretending
to be 2026. Floor 3 appears on a monitor at 01:00 and on a staircase at 04:00. The
manager's office is empty and always was: a desk, a chair, a gooseneck microphone, and a
wall of staff rotas going back to 1986 with your crew's names already typed on this
week's. MALIK's tag is on the floor outside the door, dropped mid-run.

**Night 5 — Closing.** He knows what you know. The closing routine — lights off floor by
floor, tags returned, shutter down, clock wound forward to 6:00 — is the thing that was
never done in 1998, and doing it is both the win condition and an act of mercy toward
something that has spent fifty-five years unable to go off shift.

## 9. Endings

> **The win condition is the accusation.** Getting to 06:00 is not enough; the crew has to
> name him, to a detective, with evidence. The full design is in
> [`09-the-investigation.md`](09-the-investigation.md), including the seven suspects and
> what each wrong answer costs.

### Standard — "Closing Time" *(correct accusation)*
The hands reach 6:00 and the clock strikes for the first time since 1971. The fluorescents
go out in banks, back of house first. Dawn comes through the atrium glass, which is filthy,
and that detail matters — this is not a beautiful sunrise, it is an ordinary grey morning
in a town called Northmoor. The four are at the entrance in their 1998 uniforms. Nora
raises a hand. They walk out.

Last shot: the PA indicator light outside an empty office on a floor that does not exist,
still lit.

### True — "Elias Wren" *(correct accusation, all four tags, all five tapes)*
Naming him correctly wins. Naming him correctly while holding Tape 5 does something else.

Instead of speaking the name, the crew put his own 1971 reel through the PA. He hears
himself say *"somebody's got to keep it open"* in a voice that still had somewhere to be.

He does not fight that. There is nothing to hold off for forty seconds, and the clock
strikes six on its own. The four at the entrance are five. He is at the back, in a stationmaster's coat,
and he waits for the others to go first, because that is what you do at the end of a shift.

The PA light is off.

### Wrong accusation
He takes it as a resignation letter and does not accept it. Accusing Halvard, Kask, Vale or
"the building" restarts Night 5, harder — he knows you are guessing now. Accusing **Walt or
Nora** is the worst ending in the game: you have accused a missing person of their own
disappearance, in a building that files paperwork, and their shade does not leave with the
others.

Evidence is never lost on a restart. The mystery converges; it does not reset.

### Soft fail — "3:33"
If 6:00 passes with the routine unfinished, every clock in the building snaps to 3:33,
the music restarts from the top of the tape, and the Night Manager says, gently:

> "Don't worry about it. We'll go again tomorrow."

The night restarts. In the staff room there are now more name tags on the hook than there
were, and they have your crew's names on them.

## 10. Part 2 hook

Over the credits, the walkie finds a channel it was not tuned to.

> "Hi. I hear you're good at nights.
> Hotel Winterhaven needs staff — full board, live in, starts tonight.
> Don't worry about the hours. We'll sort the hours out when you get here.
> See you tonight."

Lantern Staffing placed your crew at Northlight. Lantern Staffing has been placing crews
since 1989. Somebody at Lantern Staffing knows exactly what they are filling, and has a
list of buildings.

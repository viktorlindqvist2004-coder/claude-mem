# The cast, and where each of them is in the game

> Ten people. Two of them speak, four of them are on tape, three exist only in
> paperwork, and one of them is the answer. Every one of them is reachable in
> the built game — this is the list of where.

---

## The living

### The Night Manager — *the voice on the tannoy*

He is warm, he is useful, and he is correct about everything for four nights.
That is the construction: the horror is not that he sounds sinister, it is that
he never does.

He is a railway announcer, so the cadence is the tell and not the timbre. Every
single thing he says is a station announcement with the nouns changed.

| where | |
| --- | --- |
| Every night, from 22:00 | 64 lines, `NightScript.NIGHT1`–`NIGHT5` |
| The camera room | `PASpeaker`, the thing his voice comes out of |
| Floor 3, the manager's office | The gooseneck microphone, the chair pushed in, and nobody in the room |
| Night 1, 03:00 | **The Contradiction.** Rule 7: ask him the time. He will not tell you |
| Night 4, 04:30 | The rota, typed, with your names on it, in a room with no power |

## The answer

### Elias Wren — *Assistant Stationmaster, Northmoor Station, 1930–1971*

Wound the station clock by hand every night for forty-one years. Died at the
foot of it in 1971, fifteen years before the Galleria opened, with no connection
to the mall whatsoever — which is exactly why nobody has ever looked at him.

Halvard bought the movement at auction in 1986 and did not restore it. He cased
it in and bolted a digital display to the front, so the dedication plate that
names Wren has been facing inwards, unreadable, for forty years. The mall did to
the clock exactly what the Night Manager does to himself.

It has been announcing the trains ever since. It just calls them shops now.

| where | |
| --- | --- |
| Behind the clock display panel, Night 5 | The dedication plate. Prising the panel off is an act, not a glance |
| Inside the clock case | Tape 5, which says the name out loud |
| Night 4, the PA logbook | An entry every night since 1986. Each one eleven minutes long, 03:22 to 03:33 |
| Night 4, the microphone | A cable grey with dust except for a bright band where a hand has been, in the same place, for forty years |
| The last train | Left Northmoor at 03:33. He struck the bell once for every minute past the half hour so the platform knew how long they had. That is why there are thirty-three |

**Supported by** C18 (the auction receipt), C19 (the PA logbook), C21 (the
dedication plate), C22 (the voice log). Calder takes any three. She has no set
of three for anybody else, which makes working down the board a bad strategy
rather than a forbidden one.

# Concept Art Briefs

Six style frames that establish the look, written from
[`05-art-direction.md`](05-art-direction.md) and [`10-fear-design.md`](10-fear-design.md).

These are **prompts, not screenshots.** Nothing has been built yet. Paste them into whatever
image generator you have access to — they are written to be model-agnostic. Every one is
16:9, photoreal, cinematic, 35mm grain, and crushed blacks, because those four things are the
game's visual thesis.

If a generator keeps lifting the shadows, add *"extremely underexposed, 90% of the frame is
pure black, do not brighten"* — every model fights you on darkness.

---

## 1. The Wing
*The single most important frame. If this one works, the game works.*

> Photoreal cinematic still, no people. Interior of a huge abandoned 1986 shopping mall
> corridor at night, 110 metres long, general lighting switched off. Almost entirely black.
> The only light comes from three surviving neon shopfront signs far down the wing in
> saturated magenta and cyan, plus a small red illuminated EXIT sign on the emergency circuit
> near camera. Polished terrazzo floor, wet-mopped and mirror-like, doubling every neon sign
> in a long reflection stretching toward the lens. Beige laminate shopfronts, brushed steel
> handrails, brown carpet tile, smoked glass balustrades, 1980s Nordic municipal
> architecture, nothing refurbished since 1994. Dusty air, faint volumetric haze around each
> sign, heavy atmospheric depth. Crushed blacks, high contrast, desaturated everywhere except
> the neon. Shot on 35mm, long exposure, slight film grain, vignette. Eerie, empty, enormous.

## 2. The Atrium and the Great Clock

> Photoreal cinematic still, no people. The four-storey glass-roofed atrium of an abandoned
> 1986 shopping mall at night, seen from the ground floor looking up. Dominating the centre is
> an enormous antique brass railway station turret clock mounted on a plinth, its glass dial
> cracked with a hairline fracture, hands stopped at 3:33. Tarnished brass, ornate 1904
> ironwork, completely out of place among 1980s mall architecture. Around it: a drained and
> capped concrete fountain, dead escalators, four levels of empty balconies with smoked glass
> balustrades receding upward into darkness. Almost no light: a faint cold storm glow through
> the filthy glass roof far above, a scattering of small red EXIT signs on each balcony level,
> one distant amber neon sign. Deep shadows, crushed blacks, dust motes, volumetric haze.
> Photoreal, cinematic, wide angle, 35mm film grain. Awe and dread.

## 3. The Contour Reveal
*Tests the hardest rule in the design: he is never resolvable.*

> Photoreal cinematic still, first-person point of view of a night security worker holding a
> torch in a pitch-black abandoned shopping mall corridor. A single warm 2900K flashlight
> beam, a 60-degree cone, cuts into the darkness, volumetric through dusty air, illuminating a
> strip of polished terrazzo floor and the edge of a shuttered shopfront. At the very outer
> falloff of the beam, forty metres away where the light is too weak to resolve detail, the
> partial silhouette of a standing adult figure in a long coat is barely suggested — only a
> shoulder and the side of a head, no face, no detail, ambiguous, could be a mannequin or a
> coat stand. A small red EXIT sign glows dimly on the wall behind it. Everything else is
> absolute black. High contrast, crushed shadows, heavy grain, lens dirt catching the beam,
> vignette. Deeply unsettling, grounded, not supernatural. No visible monster.

## 4. The Window Display
*Night 2, and the eyes mechanic hiding among legitimate glass eyes.*

> Photoreal cinematic still, no living people. A dark unlit clothing shop window display
> inside an abandoned 1986 shopping mall at night. Behind the glass stand eight faceless
> retail mannequins in matte injection-moulded plastic with visible joint seams and glass
> eyes, crowded closer together than a display should be, several facing slightly the wrong
> way. One mannequin at the front wears a distinctive red collar that does not match the
> others. A handheld torch beam rakes across them from the left, catching the sheen on the
> plastic and returning two tiny retroreflective points of light from a pair of glass eyes at
> the back of the shop. Reflections of a distant magenta neon sign smear across the window
> glass. Otherwise near total darkness. Photoreal, high contrast, crushed blacks, 35mm grain,
> shallow depth of field. Quietly terrifying.

## 5. EXIT
*The mood key. Most of the game looks like this.*

> Photoreal cinematic still, no people, minimal composition. Almost the entire frame is
> absolute black. In the upper left, a single small illuminated red EXIT sign glows on the
> wall of an abandoned shopping mall, its red light spilling weakly onto a section of concrete
> ceiling, a fire door frame, and a few metres of polished terrazzo floor below, where it
> reflects as a soft red smear. Dust in the air catches a trace of the red glow. Nothing else
> is visible or identifiable. Extremely dark, high contrast, desaturated apart from the red,
> heavy film grain, vignette, long exposure look. Lonely, oppressive, enormous unseen space
> implied beyond the frame.

## 6. The Rules

> Photoreal close-up cinematic still, no people. A sheet of yellowed lined A4 paper taped with
> browned sellotape inside the open door of a grimy 1980s metal staff locker. The page is
> covered in handwritten slanted blue ballpoint that skips, a numbered list of rules, with two
> entries thickly crossed out and the bottom of the page torn away. The handwriting is cramped
> and urgent. Beside it in the locker hangs a hi-vis tabard and a small enamel staff name
> badge. Lit only by a flickering aged fluorescent tube overhead that has drifted pink with
> age, casting hard shadows in a dark cinderblock staff room. Peeling paint, grime, scuffs,
> 1998 aesthetic, nothing refurbished. Photoreal, shallow depth of field, 35mm grain, high
> contrast. Quietly ominous.

## Frames worth adding later

- **Floor 3** — bare screed, no units, no signage, no sound. The only truly silent place.
- **The camera room** — a wall of CCTV monitors, one of them labelled 3.
- **The office** — an empty desk, a gooseneck microphone, and a wall of staff rotas going back
  to 1986.
- **Closing Time** — the four at the entrance in 1998 uniforms, grey northern morning through
  filthy glass. Deliberately unglamorous.
